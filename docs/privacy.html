1. Who we are and what this covers

This policy explains the data practices of CLIP TRIBUNE, a Windows desktop video editor, and this project website. The project is maintained by matthew-mbn. Privacy contact: chippolinno622@gmail.com.

Local editing does not require a CLIP TRIBUNE account. The maintainer does not operate a cloud library for your videos or receive your local projects through the app. Online features connect from your computer to the services you choose.

2. Data stored on your computer

The app stores imported or downloaded media, advertising assets you provide, rendered clips, thumbnails, subtitles, filenames and paths, editing settings, project and job history, errors and diagnostic logs. It also stores the account and API data described below.

Live recording stores the stream URL, title, recording times and local media segments. Automatic clipping analyzes completed segments on your computer and retains generated clips. The recorded parts remain until you delete them. Clips are not published by default. If you explicitly enable an experimental automatic TikTok series before recording, the app stores your account selection, visibility, caption template, interval, limit and authorization. It sends future generated clips and these settings to TikTok without asking for each clip. An enabled series may resume after restarting the app; stop the series to revoke further sending.

The Windows EXE normally uses %LOCALAPPDATA%\CLIP TRIBUNE\storage. Running from source normally uses the project's storage folder. Advanced settings can change the storage or database location. Files and history remain until you remove them; closing or uninstalling the EXE does not automatically erase this folder.

The app has no built-in usage analytics, advertising trackers or automatic crash reporting to the maintainer. The advertising feature overlays media that you supply; it is not an ad network.

3. YouTube, TikTok and downloads

YouTube: CLIP TRIBUNE uses YouTube API Services. With your authorization, it receives OAuth tokens, your channel ID, channel name and channel statistics. It sends videos and the titles, descriptions, tags and publication settings you select, and stores upload IDs, progress and processing results locally. Trend searches send your search and region parameters to YouTube and store returned public video metadata and statistics locally.

TikTok: with your authorization, the app receives OAuth tokens, your app-specific account ID and display name. It queries creator information and posting options when needed, sends the selected video and posting settings, and stores upload/publish IDs, results and history locally. Upload-to-inbox and Direct Post use the permissions you authorize.

Login occurs on the platform's authorization page in your browser. CLIP TRIBUNE does not ask for your Google or TikTok password. A task you explicitly schedule can run later while the app is running. The destination platform processes the uploaded content under its own rules.

Downloads: downloading a supported YouTube or Twitch video sends requests to that platform and its media servers. They receive your IP address and normal connection information. The desktop downloader does not import your browser's cookies.

Read the Google Privacy Policy, TikTok Privacy Policy applicable to your region, and Twitch Privacy Notice.

4. Optional speech and AI services

If you configure a speech service and enable subtitles, the app sends the source media file to your configured speech endpoint to obtain a transcript. For live clips, this can be a window assembled from recorded segments. A cloud endpoint therefore receives that media; a service you run locally processes it at that local endpoint.

If you configure an AI metadata endpoint and model, metadata generation sends the topic, selection evidence and available transcript to that endpoint. This request does not attach the video file. The endpoint also receives its configured authentication credential.

These endpoints are empty by default. Review your chosen provider's privacy and retention terms before enabling it. The maintainer does not receive these requests, sell the data, use it for advertising or use it to train models. Independently chosen providers have their own practices.

5. Credentials and security

OAuth tokens and secrets saved through the app's settings are encrypted in the local database. The encryption key is a separate file in the storage folder. This is not full-disk encryption: someone with access to the database and key can decrypt the secrets. Media and ordinary project records are not encrypted by this feature. Credentials you place in a .env file remain in that file as plain text.

Protect your Windows account, backups and storage directory. Do not publish tokens, OAuth credential files, the database or encryption key. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

6. Disconnecting, retention and deletion

Use Disconnect on the corresponding publishing page to revoke the app's platform access. YouTube disconnect removes the local channel record and tokens; TikTok disconnect clears the tokens. Some account identifiers and publication history can remain locally, especially TikTok history. Disconnecting is not a complete local-data wipe.

You can also revoke Google access through Google's security settings and TikTok access through TikTok's connected-app controls. Revocation on a platform stops authorized access but does not remotely erase files on your computer.

  1. For a full local reset, first save any videos you want to keep outside the app's data folder.
  2. Disconnect linked accounts, stop pending work and completely exit CLIP TRIBUNE.
  3. Delete the app's storage folder at the location above. This removes the local database, tokens, history and media stored there. Also remove any separate .env, downloaded OAuth credentials, custom data locations or backups that you no longer want.

The app cannot erase separately exported copies or platform-hosted posts through this reset. Manage published videos in YouTube or TikTok. The maintainer cannot remotely access or delete your local files. Email the contact below for help or to request deletion of information you supplied to the maintainer.

7. Website visits and support messages

This static website uses no analytics scripts, tracking pixels, third-party fonts or cookies set by the project. GitHub Pages hosts it. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security and processes hosting requests under the GitHub Privacy Statement. Following links to GitHub or other services is subject to their own policies.

If you email chippolinno622@gmail.com, the maintainer and email service receive your address, message and any attachments you choose to send. They are used to answer the request and retained only as needed to handle it and meet applicable obligations. You may request deletion. Do not send passwords or tokens. Public GitHub issues are visible to everyone; use email for private matters.

8. Your choices and policy updates

You can use local editing without connecting platforms or AI providers. Depending on your location, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to processing of your personal information, or obtain a copy. Contact chippolinno622@gmail.com about information held by the maintainer; contact the relevant platform for information it holds. You may also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

The project is not intended for children under 13. Platform age requirements also apply. If you believe a child sent personal information to the maintainer, contact us.

Updates to these practices will be described on this page with a revised date. This policy describes the current desktop app; it does not promise approval by any platform or certification under a particular privacy law.
